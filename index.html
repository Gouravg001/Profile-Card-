Italian Authentic Pasta

White sauce pasta is a comforting and creamy dish made by tossing tender pasta into a velvety Béchamel sauce. Rooted in classic European cooking, it combines butter, flour, and milk with aromatic garlic, herbs, and melted cheese. Each bite delivers a rich, smooth texture and a savory flavor that makes it a family favorite around the world.

Popular Styles of Creamy White Sauce Pasta

  • Classic Penne Béchamel: Penne pasta tossed in a smooth, butter-and-milk white sauce with bell peppers, corn, and herbs.
  • Fettuccine Alfredo: Rich parmesan and cream-coated flat ribbon pasta, often garnished with fresh parsley or grilled chicken.
  • Cafe-Style Veggie White Pasta: Macaroni or penne smothered in a thick cheese-slice-infused white sauce with sautéed garlic, onions, and tri-color capsicum.

Making a lovely Italian Pasta

Ingredients(Classic Egg Pasta):

  • All-purpose flour: 300g (about 2 cups)
  • Italian fine semolina flour: 200g (about 1.3 cups)
  • Fresh whole eggs: 5 large eggs (roughly 1 egg per 100g of flour)
  • Salt: For the boiling water (do not add salt or oil directly to the dough)

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Make the Flour Well: Combine the flour and semolina on a clean wooden board or large mixing bowl. Form a mound and make a wide, deep well in the center.
  2. Add the Eggs: Crack the whole eggs directly into the center of the well. Using a fork, gently beat the eggs together, then slowly pull in flour from the inner walls of the well, mixing until a thick paste forms.
  3. Knead the Dough: Use your hands or a bench scraper to fold the remaining flour into the wet mixture until it comes together into a shaggy ball. Knead the dough firmly with the palms of your hands, pushing forward and folding back for about 10 minutes. The dough should turn smooth, elastic, and spring back when poked. (If it feels too dry, wet your hands slightly and keep kneading).
  4. Rest the Dough: Wrap the dough tightly in plastic wrap or cover it with an inverted bowl and let it rest at room temperature for 30 minutes so the gluten relaxes.
  5. Roll and Cut: Cut the dough into manageable quarters. Flatten one piece and run it through a pasta machine starting at the widest setting, folding and repeating, then gradually decrease the thickness until you reach your desired thinness. Alternatively, roll it out evenly with a rolling pin. Cut into your preferred shape (tagliatelle, pappardelle, or fettuccine) using a knife or machine attachment.
  6. Cook the Pasta: Bring a large pot of water to a rolling boil and salt it heavily (it should taste like the ocean). Fresh pasta cooks very quickly—usually 2 to 3 minutes—or until it floats to the top. Toss it straight into your favorite warm sauce with a splash of starchy pasta water to finish (mantecare).
